# Creative Feature: Weak-Spot Gap Radar

## Overview

The **Weak-Spot Gap Radar** is Recon's signature diagnostic feature. While traditional flashcard apps track vanity metrics like "total cards reviewed" or "deck completion percentage," the Weak-Spot Gap Radar links active recall performance directly back to the employer's Job Description requirements (`must-have` and `nice-to-have` criteria).

It guarantees that a candidate who masters 50 secondary flashcards but ignores a core job requirement is never misled into feeling "ready."

---

## 1. Architectural Motivation: Why Card Counts Fail

### The Illusion of Competence
In standard interview prep platforms, a candidate might review 40 flashcards and see:
> *"80% of flashcards mastered! Great job!"*

However, in reality:
- 30 of those flashcards were about general trivia or soft skills.
- The role's critical requirement (**"Distributed consensus & Raft protocol"**) had 5 flashcards associated with it, and the candidate reviewed **none of them**.
- The candidate walks into the interview with a fatal blind spot and fails the technical round.

### The Requirement-First Paradigm
The Weak-Spot Gap Radar reverses the relationship between flashcards and progress:
- Every flashcard generated by Recon's AI pipeline is linked to one or more `target_requirements` (e.g., `req_distributed_systems`, `req_kafka`).
- Progress is measured not by total cards, but by **verified competency per requirement**.
- Unpracticed requirements are flagged prominently before the interview date.

---

## 2. Mathematical Modeling & The Cardinality Assumption

### 2.1 The Readiness Formula
For any given requirement $R$, its readiness percentage is formulated as:

$$\text{readiness}(R) = \frac{\sum_{c \in \text{Cards}(R)} \text{lastConfidence}(c)}{|\text{Cards}(R)| \times 3} \times 100$$

Where:
- $\text{Cards}(R)$ is the set of all flashcards linked to requirement $R$.
- $\text{lastConfidence}(c) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$:
  - If card $c$ has been rated, its latest confidence rating is used (`1`, `2`, or `3`).
  - If card $c$ has **never been practiced**, its rating is strictly **$0$**.
- The denominator $|\text{Cards}(R)| \times 3$ represents the maximum possible confidence score across all linked cards.

### 2.2 Cardinality Assumption Note
The confidence rating system captures ordinal human feedback:
1. **Shaky** (`1`): Candidate struggled to recall.
2. **Good** (`2`): Candidate recalled with minor hesitation.
3. **Mastered** (`3`): Candidate recalled fluently.

In classic measurement theory, ordinal ratings (`shaky < good < mastered`) do not possess strictly uniform distance intervals. In the Weak-Spot Gap Radar, we make a deliberate, pragmatic **cardinal linear assumption**:
- Rating `1` contributes $1/3 \approx 33.3\%$ toward full mastery of that card.
- Rating `2` contributes $2/3 \approx 66.7\%$ toward full mastery of that card.
- Rating `3` contributes $3/3 = 100\%$ toward full mastery of that card.

#### Modeling Tradeoffs & Edge Cases
- **Single-Card Edge Case**: If a requirement has only 1 linked card and the candidate rates it `1 (Shaky)`, the requirement displays $33.3\%$ readiness. This accurately reflects that the candidate knows the concept exists but cannot articulate it reliably.
- **Why Not Binary (Pass/Fail)?**: A binary model discards the nuanced distinction between "I've never seen this concept" ($0\%$), "I know this roughly" ($33\%-66\%$), and "I know this cold" ($100\%$).
- **User Intuitiveness**: Candidates immediately comprehend that 3 out of 3 stars equals 100%, 2 out of 3 equals 67%, and 1 out of 3 equals 33%.

## 3. The Zero-Overstatement Guarantee

The fundamental design constraint of the Weak-Spot Gap Radar is that **readiness can never be overstated**.

### 3.1 Mathematical Proof of Non-Inflation
Suppose a requirement has $N$ linked flashcards ($N \ge 1$). A candidate practices $k$ cards ($k < N$) and receives a perfect score of $3$ on all $k$ cards.

Under an unprincipled averaging scheme (averaging only practiced cards):
$$\text{readiness}_{\text{flawed}} = \frac{k \times 3}{k \times 3} = 100\% \quad (\text{false mastery})$$

Under Recon's Zero-Overstatement Formula:
$$\text{readiness}_{\text{Recon}} = \frac{k \times 3 + (N - k) \times 0}{N \times 3} = \frac{k}{N} \times 100\% < 100\%$$

The unpracticed cards contribute $0$ to the numerator but still count fully in the denominator ($N \times 3$). Mastery cannot be achieved until **every linked card** has been reviewed and mastered.

### 3.2 Zero-Linked Requirement Safeguard
If an extracted JD requirement has no associated flashcards ($|\text{Cards}(R)| = 0$):
$$\text{readiness}(R) = 0\%$$

The system strictly refuses to divide by zero or assign artificial readiness to untested competencies.

## 4. Reconciled Diagnostic States: Danger Zone vs. Partial Practice

To prevent alert fatigue while maintaining high-urgency warnings for genuine blind spots, the radar distinguishes between two severity tiers on `must-have` requirements:

```
                                  Requirement Priority
                                     /             \
                               [ must ]          [ nice ]
                                 /    \              |
            practicedCount == 0?       practiced?   Readiness % (Info)
                 /          \             |
             [ YES ]      [ NO ]     Readiness %
                |            |
         🚨 DANGER ZONE   readiness < 50% || unpracticed > 0?
         (Red Banner)        /          \
                         [ YES ]       [ NO ]
                            |             |
                     ⚠️ PARTIALLY     ✅ ON TRACK
                     UNPREPARED       (Emerald)
                     (Amber Pill)
```

### 4.1 Danger Zone (`isDangerZone`)
- **Criteria**:
  1. Requirement priority is strictly `'must'`.
  2. Total linked cards $> 0$.
  3. Practiced card count is exactly $0$ (`practicedCardCount === 0`).
- **Clinical Meaning**: The candidate has a **total blind spot** on a mandatory qualification required by the employer.
- **UX Treatment**: High-urgency **Danger Zone Alert Banner** rendered above the radar cards:
  - Background: `bg-red-950/40 border border-red-500/40`
  - Icon: Pulsing `AlertTriangle` in `text-red-400`
  - Actionable Copy: Names the exact unpracticed mandatory requirements and directs the candidate to filter by *"Unpracticed Only"*.

### 4.2 Partially Unprepared (`isPartiallyUnprepared`)
- **Criteria**:
  1. Requirement priority is `'must'`.
  2. Candidate has practiced at least one card (`practicedCardCount > 0`).
  3. Either $\text{readiness} < 50\%$ or unpracticed cards remain (`practicedCardCount < totalLinkedCards`).
- **Clinical Meaning**: The candidate has started practicing this requirement, but has not reached sufficient mastery or has skipped some linked questions.
- **UX Treatment**: Requirement card displays an **Amber Warning Pill**:
  - Badge: `Needs Attention` (`bg-amber-500/20 text-amber-300 border-amber-500/30`)
  - Progress bar colored in amber (`bg-amber-500`) instead of emerald.

### 4.3 On Track (`isOnTrack`)
- **Criteria**: $\text{readiness} \ge 50\%$ and all linked cards have been rehearsed at least once.
- **UX Treatment**: Requirement card displays emerald progress bar with clear mastery breakdown.

### 4.4 Nice-to-Have Requirements
- Requirements tagged as `'nice'` in the job extraction pipeline display informational readiness percentages but **never trigger the Danger Zone Banner**, preventing false alarms for non-essential qualifications.

## 5. UI Integration & Real-Time Feedback

The Weak-Spot Gap Radar is rendered directly inside the **Flashcards & Practice** tab of the kit workspace:

1. **Overall Interview Readiness Gauge**: A radial / progress summary showing aggregate readiness across all requirements.
2. **Danger Zone Sticky Alert**: Visible immediately whenever `dangerZoneRequirements.length > 0`.
3. **Requirement Diagnostic Cards**:
   - Priority badge (`Must-Have` in slate-200 / `Nice-to-Have` in slate-400).
   - Animated progress bar reflecting $\text{readiness}(R)\%$.
   - Card tally: `"X/Y cards rehearsed"`.
   - Direct button to switch the queue to `"Shaky Cards First"` or `"Unpracticed Only"`.
